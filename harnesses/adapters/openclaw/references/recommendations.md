# Recommendations

This document expands the `## Recommendation Format` section of `SKILL.md`.

## Output structure

A completed recommendation contains:

1. A short statement of the understood need.
2. Any assumptions the user skipped or left unknown, labeled in plain language.
3. Two to four qualified options, when available.
4. For each option:
   - Observed price, currency, and observation date.
   - Seller or manufacturer.
   - Why it fits, anchored to the criteria and `score_breakdown`.
   - Important trade-offs.
   - Unverified details.
   - Direct source links.
5. A concise comparison explaining why the top option ranks first.
6. A reminder that price and stock are volatile and should be verified before purchase.

## Trade-off language

Trade-offs are honest, specific, and short. They cover limitations, durability concerns, fit caveats, missing evidence, and conflicts between sources. They never soften a real problem with marketing language. They label unverified claims as unverified.

## When fewer than two qualify

Do not manufacture a shortlist when fewer than two candidates meet the evidence threshold. Present the qualified result, if any, and explain which hard constraints eliminated the rest. Offer to relax a constraint if the user wants more options.

## Language and length

Match the active messaging surface. Use concise language on chat surfaces and structured language on longer surfaces. Always include direct source links. Never substitute fabricated prices, availability, ratings, or specifications.

## Volatility reminder

Every recommendation includes a short reminder that prices and stock change and should be verified before purchase. The reminder does not replace the source links; it sits next to them.
